Tell it where you play. After that it notices when you arrive at the court and when you leave, and writes the session down — while your phone is in your bag and the app is closed.

In review with Apple. Nearly there.

It runs by itself

Pick the courts you play at and draw a circle round them. That is the whole setup. From then on a session starts when you arrive and ends when you leave — no timer to remember, and nothing to tap when you would rather be warming up.

When it misses one, and occasionally it will, you can start, correct or add a session by hand. It counts exactly the same.

App Store privacy label

Data Not Collected

There is no account and no sign-up. There is no Pickleball Log server, because there is nothing to send anywhere. No analytics, no advertising, no tracking — your history is a file on your iPhone, backed up the way the rest of your phone is, and exportable whenever you like, photos included.

That is not a promise about how we handle your data. It is that we never receive it.

The privacy policy has the full detail, including the two places the app talks to Apple.

A look at it

Before you buy

Pickleball Log needs Always location access to notice sessions while it is closed — that is the whole idea, and it is why the app can log a session you never opened it for. It only ever looks at the courts you add. It has no interest in anywhere else you go, and nothing it sees leaves the phone.

Battery cost is small: this uses the same geofencing iOS already runs for reminders, not continuous GPS.

iPhone only. There is a home screen widget, and it can tell you what it recorded when you leave a court.
